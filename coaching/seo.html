The Problem With Most SEO Teams

It's rarely knowledge. Most in-house teams know what a canonical tag does and can read a Core Web Vitals report. What they can't do is rank forty findings by business impact and defend the top three to a CFO.

So the work becomes a treadmill of low-impact tasks that are easy to justify and easy to complete — alt text, meta descriptions, a blog post a week — while the three structural problems that actually cap the site's ceiling go untouched for years because nobody has the authority or the confidence to escalate them.

Coaching fixes the judgment, not the knowledge. And judgment is the part a course cannot teach, because it only exists in contact with your specific site, your specific constraints, and your specific internal politics.

Skills Your Team Develops

01
Search Intent Mapping

Match content to the exact intent behind each query, so every page serves a real user need and a real ranking opportunity. This is also the single biggest determinant of whether a page ever gets quoted in an AI answer.

02
Scalable Keyword Architecture

Keyword structures that grow with the site rather than keyword lists that expire after one campaign cycle. Hub-and-spoke topical coverage that compounds instead of fragmenting.

03
Technical Auditing With Judgment

Find crawl issues, indexation problems and Core Web Vitals failures — then correctly ignore most of them. Includes reading a crawl critically: I've seen a crawler report thirty-eight dead pages that were all live and healthy.

04
Diagnosing Traffic Loss

Separating a ranking drop from a click-through collapse from a seasonal dip. These look identical on a dashboard and have completely different fixes, and getting it wrong costs a quarter.

05
Stakeholder Reporting

Communicate performance in business terms. Stop defending rankings, start showing revenue impact, and build the internal credibility that unlocks more budget.

How Sessions Are Structured

First session · 90 minutes

Review of the site, the Search Console account, and whatever the team is currently working on. Output is a ranked list of what actually matters, which is usually shorter and more uncomfortable than expected.

Common Questions

Is this a course?

No. There's no fixed curriculum and no recordings. Sessions are led by whatever is actually broken in your campaigns this week. If your team already knows something, we skip it.

Who should be in the room?

Whoever executes. Coaching a marketing director who then relays it secondhand wastes half the value. The person running the crawl should be the person in the session.

Does this cover AI search too?

Yes, and it has to. The pages that earn citations in AI answers are largely the pages that were built well for search in the first place — but the measurement is completely different, and a team that only watches rankings will miss a visibility collapse entirely.

How long before we don't need you?

That's the goal and it's usually two to four months for a competent team. If an engagement runs longer than that without the team getting more independent, something is wrong with how it's structured.
