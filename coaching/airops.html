What AirOps Does

AirOps is the execution half of AI search. Where Profound tells you where you stand, AirOps builds and refreshes the content that changes it — including Quill, an agent that develops briefs against strategic goals and runs production with humans in the review loop.

It also covers the two things content teams systematically under-do: refresh of pages that already have authority, and offsite strategy — influencing the third-party pages that AI engines cite when they answer questions about your category.

That second one matters more than it sounds. If a GEO audit shows the engines answering your category question by citing a competitor's listicle, no amount of publishing on your own domain fixes it. Offsite is the lever, and it's the one most content tools ignore entirely.

How We Run It

01
Audit What You Already Have

Before generating anything. Most sites have more recoverable value in existing pages than in new ones, and publishing on top of a thin-content problem makes it worse.

02
Configure Brand Context

Voice, positioning, proof points, claim boundaries. This is what separates output that sounds like you from output that sounds like everyone using the same model.

03
Build The Briefing Layer

Real briefs: intent classification, required entities, internal links, word-count targets grounded in what actually ranks. My content brief method is published openly.

04
Set Refresh Before Net-New

Identify decaying pages with existing equity and fix those first. Faster returns and considerably lower risk than a publishing sprint.

05
Turn Up Volume Last

Only once governance holds and the review gate is being enforced. Scaling an unreliable process just produces failure faster.

06
Measure Against Citations

Close the loop back to Profound. Publishing volume is not the metric — getting cited is.

Common Questions

Will this just produce AI slop at scale?

That's the actual risk and it's why governance comes first. Volume without brand controls and a review gate produces content that damages the site faster than no content at all. We set the guardrails before we turn up the throughput.

Does AirOps replace our writers?

No, it changes what they do. The bottleneck moves from drafting to briefing, editing and judgment. Teams that treat it as a writer replacement get worse output; teams that treat it as leverage for good writers get considerably more.

How does content refresh fit in?

It's arguably the highest-ROI use. AirOps notes content is roughly three times more likely to be cited if it's under three months old — so systematic refresh of pages that already have authority usually beats publishing new ones.

Can you run it for us?

Yes. Done-for-you covers workflow build, brand context configuration, and running the production cadence with human review. Coaching hands that to your team once it's stable.
