Three Layers, One Job

AI search, classic search and the marketing strategy underneath are usually sold separately and staffed separately. They are the same question asked at three altitudes: who is this for, why would they choose it, and where do they find out.

01 — GEO

AI Search Visibility

Your buyers increasingly ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI Overviews instead of scanning links. Those answers name two or three brands. Getting named is a different job from ranking, because the model assembles its answer largely from third-party sources rather than your own site — which is why publishing another page on your domain often changes nothing.

02 — SEO

Search Engine Optimization

Crawl and index diagnostics, site architecture, internal linking, Core Web Vitals and structured data. Category and product architecture for eCommerce competing against far larger budgets, bottom-of-funnel content for SaaS, and local search across Gujarat — Google Business Profile, citation consistency and location pages that earn their place.

03 — Digital

Marketing Strategy

Positioning, audience research and channel strategy — for products entering a market and for companies that launched and then flattened. Plus the measurement layer: analytics that answers which work produced which revenue, rather than a dashboard nobody acts on.

Why Splitting Them Fails

Most flat-growth problems turn out to be positioning problems wearing a channel costume. The ads underperform, so the team tests creative, then tests channels, then blames the market — when the actual issue is that nobody can say clearly why a buyer should choose this over the alternative.

AI search makes that worse, not better. When a model is asked who the best provider is, it assembles an answer from what third parties say about you. If your positioning is vague, the sources describing you are vague, and you do not get named. You cannot fix that with a content calendar.

So the work runs in one direction: get the positioning right, make the site structurally able to rank, then make sure the sources AI engines actually cite have something accurate to say about you.

How An Engagement Runs

A diagnostic call first. Thirty minutes, free, no sales script. You describe the business and what marketing is currently doing for it; I tell you what I would look at first and whether the problem is even search.

Then an audit. Technical state, content and keyword coverage, competitor positions, and a real prompt set run against the AI engines to see who currently gets named for the questions your buyers ask.

Then a roadmap you could hand to someone else. Sequenced by expected impact with the reasoning attached, so it survives you changing your mind about who executes it.

Reporting carries baselines and timeframes. When something moves the wrong way, that gets reported too.
