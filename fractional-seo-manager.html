The Gap This Fills

There's a stage most companies hit where SEO clearly matters, there's budget for it, and there may already be people doing the work — but nobody senior owns the direction. The result is a team that stays busy and a channel that stays flat.

A full-time head of SEO or SEO director is an expensive hire, hard to assess if nobody internal has done the job, and frequently more capacity than the problem requires. A fractional arrangement gives you the seniority without the permanent cost or the hiring risk.

The difference from consulting is ownership. A consultant hands you a recommendation. A fractional head owns whether it happens, defends the roadmap when a stakeholder wants something else, and is accountable when a quarter goes badly.

The First Ninety Days

01
Audit The Function, Not Just The Site

What's being worked on, who decides, where the work gets stuck, and what leadership believes about search. The organisational answer usually explains more than the technical one.

02
Establish The Real Baseline

Technical health, Search Console reality, competitive position, and AI visibility. Frequently the first time anyone has stated the position honestly.

03
Set A Defensible Roadmap

Ranked, with reasoning, and short enough to be remembered. Agreed with leadership so priorities don't get relitigated every month.

04
Install The Operating Cadence

Which review runs weekly, which monthly, wired to real data sources and routed to people who act on them. The cadence design is published openly.

05
Build Toward Handover

From day one. Either the team grows into ownership or you hire a permanent lead — and I'll help you assess candidates, because most companies interview badly for this role.

Common Questions

How is this different from a consultant engagement?

A consultant advises and leaves. A fractional head of SEO owns the outcome — the roadmap, the cadence, the team's priorities, and the conversation with leadership when results are questioned. You're buying accountability, not just expertise.

How many days a month?

Typically two to six, depending on team size and how much is in flight. Enough to own direction and unblock people; not so much that you're paying senior rates for execution someone else should do.

Do you manage our existing team?

Functionally yes — priorities, review, and capability development — without the line-management overhead. Most teams need direction and a senior reviewer far more than they need another pair of hands.

What happens when the engagement ends?

That's the point. Either your team has grown into the ownership, or you've hired a permanent lead and I've helped you interview for them. An engagement that runs forever has failed at its actual job.

Does this cover AI search?

Yes, and it's increasingly where the leadership conversation goes. Someone senior has to own the answer to 'why aren't we showing up in ChatGPT', and it's rarely a question the existing team is equipped to handle.
