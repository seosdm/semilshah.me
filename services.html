How This Is Organised

Most consultants publish a list of tactics. This is organised by what you actually need from an outside person — diagnosis, ownership, direction, or capability — because that's the decision you're really making.

Everything spans both surfaces of modern search. Classic ranking in Google, and generative search — being named when someone asks ChatGPT, Perplexity or AI Overviews the same question. Those are related but distinct problems and most providers still only solve the first.

The methods aren't secret. The audit skills, briefing formats and operating cadences used in client work are published openly on GitHub, with real worked examples on real data. You can read exactly how the work is done before deciding whether to buy it.

Common Questions

Which of these do I actually need?

If search matters and nobody senior owns it, start with fractional. If you need diagnosis, start with consulting. If you have a team who are capable but stuck, start with coaching. If you don't know, that's a fifteen-minute conversation rather than a purchase decision.

Do you work with agencies or direct clients?

Both. Agencies typically want delivery systems and tooling that scale across a book of accounts. Direct clients want the search function to work. Different problems, different engagements.

Is everything remote?

Yes, with Toronto-based clients seen in person when it's useful. Most of the work is with teams across North America, so timezone overlap matters more than geography.

What don't you do?

Paid media execution, volume link building, and anything that trades long-term risk for a short-term chart. I'll also decline work where the honest answer is that you don't need a consultant.
